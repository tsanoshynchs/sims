Water Property Lab

Collect the same data you would at the lab bench. Count drops on a penny for four liquids, then measure how high each one climbs a glass capillary tube.

Liquids are tinted so you can see them.
Liquid
Penny (group member)
Click the button, tap the penny, or press Space for each drop. Count them yourself. Nothing on screen keeps count for you.

Procedure

  1. Choose a liquid and a penny. Each penny is one group member's trial.
  2. Add drops one at a time and count every drop that stays on the penny.
  3. When the liquid spills over the edge, stop. Don't count the drop that made it spill.
  4. Record your count in the data table. Do 4 pennies for each liquid.
  5. Calculate the average for each liquid.

Station 1 data: drops on a penny

Average = (sum of the 4 counts) ÷ 4. Round to one decimal place.

Liquid
  1. Measure the liquid's surface from the bottom of the beaker. Record Height A.
  2. Put a clean capillary tube into the liquid at an angle.
  3. Wait 30 seconds.
  4. Rotate the tube until it's vertical. Wait 10 seconds.
  5. Measure the top of the liquid in the tube (bottom of the meniscus) from the bottom of the beaker. Record Height B.
  6. Calculate capillary rise = B − A.
  7. Discard the tube.
