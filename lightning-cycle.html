    Try this
    1. Run with the default settings and turn on "Pause at each stage." At every stop, point to where the extra electrons are.
    2. Predict: will a low cloud or a high cloud strike first? Run both and compare "Time to first strike."
    3. Choose the building with the lightning rod. What changes about where the strike connects, and how soon it happens?
    4. Turn the rising air and the ice way down. How does that change the time between strikes?
    Model check and what this model simplifies
    • Not to scale. Real thunderclouds are miles tall and hold far more charge. Amounts here are "charge units," not real measurements.
    • "Pull" is a simple stand-in. It grows with more charge and shrinks with more distance, like Coulomb's law. It is not a measured force, and this model does not calculate the electric field.
    • Air "gives way" at one fixed pull. Real air depends on more things, such as humidity and pressure.
    • Each collision moves one electron. Real clouds have enormous numbers of collisions of many kinds.
    • The lightning path is drawn randomly. Every real strike takes a different path.
    • After a strike, almost all of the extra electrons at the cloud's bottom reach the ground, and charging pauses for a moment before it starts again. The cloud's top stays positive. Real storms also have flashes inside the cloud.
    • The ground starts neutral. Only polarization and the electrons from a strike are shown.
